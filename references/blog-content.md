# Blog Content Reference

Use this reference for blog/article SEO improvements grounded in official guidance.

## Official Baselines

- Google Search Central SEO Starter Guide: create descriptive titles, helpful snippets, logical headings, useful links, and images that are relevant to nearby text.
- Google "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content": prioritize content made for people, first-hand or clearly sourced information, and transparent expertise/trust signals.
- Google Search Essentials and spam policies: avoid keyword stuffing, scaled low-value content, hidden text, misleading links, scraped content without value, and manipulative practices.
- Bing Webmaster Guidelines: align with user intent, provide clear content, avoid cloaking, keyword stuffing, link schemes, and autogenerated low-value pages.

## Review Checklist

### Search Intent and Usefulness

- Identify the main question or job the reader wants solved.
- Check whether the post answers that intent early enough.
- Prefer concrete examples, steps, tradeoffs, screenshots, data, or lived experience when relevant.
- Flag vague, generic, duplicated, or AI-sounding sections only when you can cite visible evidence.

### Title and Meta Description

- Title should accurately describe the page and distinguish it from other pages.
- Meta description should summarize the specific value of the post. Search engines may rewrite snippets.
- A keyword near the beginning can help clarity, but do not force unnatural phrasing.
- Length limits are display heuristics, not official ranking rules. Avoid needless truncation, but prioritize clarity and accuracy.

### Headings and Structure

- Use headings to expose the article structure and make scanning easier.
- Keep hierarchy logical. Do not use headings only for visual styling.
- Include target terms naturally in headings when they help readers understand the section.

### Links and Citations

- Internal links should help the reader continue the same task or understand prerequisites.
- External links should point to original, official, or high-authority sources when supporting factual claims.
- Use descriptive anchor text. Avoid vague anchors such as "click here" when context can be clearer.

### Images

- Use images that explain the post's concepts, steps, evidence, or examples.
- Place images near the related text when possible.
- Alt text should describe the image's content and role in context. Decorative images should use empty alt or equivalent.
- Captions can clarify why the image matters; they are not a place for keyword stuffing.

## What Not to Do

- Do not add paragraphs solely to hit a word count.
- Do not optimize for keyword density.
- Do not invent first-hand experience, credentials, source claims, or statistics.
- Do not add unsupported "best", "official", "guaranteed", or "rank #1" claims.
